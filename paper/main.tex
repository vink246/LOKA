\begin{abstract}
Translating the semantic reasoning capabilities of foundation models into the high-frequency control signals required for robotic systems remains a critical challenge.
Existing paradigms face significant architectural barriers: monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle to adapt to novel task combinations in long-horizon domains; offline Domain Randomization (DR) leaves a persistent deployment gap under unexpected physical alterations; and autoregressive Large Language Model (LLM) code-generation frameworks introduce latencies that are fatal to dynamic bipedal balancing.
This paper introduces LLM-Orchestrated Kinematic Adaptation (LOKA), a hybrid neuro-symbolic control architecture for real-time humanoid stabilization and gait discovery.
LOKA decouples low-frequency semantic reasoning from high-frequency physical optimization through an asynchronous dual-rate execution engine.
An intermediate Telemetry Compressor monitors motor effort and mission criteria, filtering raw high-frequency data into dense semantic trend tags.
Upon detecting internal or environmental anomalies or an operator language request, a language model acts as an online system-identification engine via a structured YAML scratchpad.
Rather than generating high-latency policy code, the orchestrator mutates the objective landscape, task targets, success criteria, and kinematic assumptions referenced by an active Model Predictive Controller (MPC).
By bridging qualitative inference with quantitative optimal control, LOKA targets zero-shot physical adaptation under structural degradation and extreme environmental transitions.
\end{abstract}
